Canada's job vacancies, industry by industry

Every other reference on this site measures what a job pays. This one measures what employers are trying to fill, from a different survey altogether: Statistics Canada's Job Vacancy and Wage Survey, which counts the jobs left open on a reference date and the wage offered for them. The page prints, for each of the country's 21 published industry sectors and for the whole economy quarter by quarter, the number of vacancies, the vacancy rate, and the average offered hourly wage — each one the survey's own figure, with the source and the date it was read.

03

Reading this page honestly

  • One survey, one file. The whole page rests on a single Statistics Canada table, 14-10-0442 from the Job Vacancy and Wage Survey, published under the Statistics Canada Open Licence. The CSV read here is 14100442-eng.zip; it hashes to 3a00cb558f53aafad7c1ee5d19ae7e61219f9fa2c76d0cad0e79c00d2287d6fb, and the table page plus the download are where that can be checked.
  • Vacant means actively sought. The survey counts a job as vacant only if the employer is taking steps to recruit someone from outside the organisation for it, so a filled post, a cancelled req or a role nobody is chasing is not in the total. It is a reading of employer demand, not a count of every opening that will ever exist.
  • Offered, not paid. The wage column is what employers advertised for the open jobs, and the survey records the bottom of a range when one is posted. The wage those same jobs actually pay once filled is a different survey's question, and it is the one the salary pages ask.
  • Unadjusted for seasonality. This is the survey's own unadjusted series, so a summer quarter and a winter quarter are not like for like. Read the twelve-period table as counts as printed, not as a seasonally-smoothed line.
  • Data-quality marks. Statistics Canada stamps each estimate with a quality letter: in the sector table A means excellent and B very good. The page prints the figure either way; the mark is the file's own.
  • A snapshot, not a posting. The table describes sectors and an economy, not a particular employer or a particular job, and nothing on it is guidance of any kind. Nothing here is drawn from the paid pack.

The survey also carries each sector's smaller pieces — sub-sectors such as computing infrastructure providers and data processing [518] inside information and cultural industries, and computer and electronic product manufacturing [334] — but this page stays at the sector level so every row is the file's own published aggregate. The CSV is free to reuse with attribution, and the link above is the whole source.
