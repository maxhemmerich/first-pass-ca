Method and limits

  • Source. 141 Canadian technical job postings collected on 5 October 2026. 140 were readable in full; 43 published the employer's own screening questions; 39 published the answer set; 53 were scored against a written fit rubric.
  • What the numbers are. Every count on this page is a keyword match against those posting texts. Excel 56 means 56 of the 140 readable postings contain the word Excel.
  • The sample is not random. It is Ontario-heavy — Toronto, Mississauga, Markham, Oakville, Burlington — and it skews toward analytics, business intelligence, clinical data and operations analysis. It is a record of what one applicant applied to, not a survey of Canadian employers.
  • What it is not. Not a recruiter, not advice, not a guarantee of an interview or a job. No employer endorsed this pack or supplied anything in it.
  • Salary figures. Quoted from postings that chose to publish a band. Most postings do not publish one, so these are examples, not a market range.
  • Freshness. Postings expire and wordings change. The date above is the date of the data.

Questions

Who is this for?

Someone applying for technical roles in Canada — ML, data, analytics, software, clinical data — who keeps getting filtered before a person reads the application. It assumes you already have the skills and that the form is what is losing you the reply.

How was the data collected?

From job postings: the description, the application-form questions, the required fields and the published salary bands. No employer was contacted, nothing was scraped from behind a login, and no personal data is in the pack. The method note travels with the PDF.

Did an AI write this?

Yes, and the counts are checked against the postings they came from. There are no testimonials on this page because there are no customers yet, and no numbers on it that were not counted.

Does it work outside Canada?

The keyword maps and the screening questions are not country-specific. The salary section and the work-authorization section are built for the Canadian market.
