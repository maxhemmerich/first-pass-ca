03

What the screening form asks before a human reads anything

  1. The asterisk. Required fields are the first screen. In this sample they are the pay box, the years-of-experience box and the skills grid.
  2. The knockout questions. Work authorization and sponsorship (12 of 43), a completed degree (6 of 43), and location or commute (5 of 43). These are single-select, and a wrong answer is not a judgement call, it is a filter.
  3. The character limit. A published cap of 200 characters for a technologies answer. A cap is a test of editing, not of knowledge.
  4. The skills grid. A row per skill, with required years and proficiency. One posting in the sample has 41 rows.
  5. The consent box. Background checks and reference release appear in 4 of 43 postings, with release wording you accept before you can submit.
  6. The published band. Seven salary bands and two hourly rates in this sample. Where one exists, it is the number to anchor to.

05

Method and limits

  • Source. 141 Canadian technical job postings collected between 24 September and 5 October 2026 — data analyst, business intelligence, data engineering, machine learning, clinical data and software roles, almost all in Ontario. 140 were readable in full; 43 published the employer's own screening questions; 39 published the answer set; 53 were scored against a written fit rubric; across the 43 there are 224 distinct question strings.
  • The sample is not random. It is one applicant's pipeline, counted — not a survey of Canadian employers and not a description of how any named company's software works internally.
  • What every number is. A keyword match against those posting texts. Excel 56 means 56 of the 140 readable postings contain the word Excel.
  • Salary figures. Quoted from postings that chose to publish a band. Most postings do not publish one, so these are examples, not a market range.
  • What this is not. Information, not advice, not legal, immigration or tax advice, and not a promise of an interview or a job. No employer endorsed this document or supplied anything in it.

Salary expectations question — job application Canada

The three things this page is measured to answer, answered from what is above. No new numbers, no advice the page does not already carry.

How often does a Canadian technical job posting ask about salary?

In this sample, 27 of the 43 postings that published the employer's screening questions ask about pay. That is the most common question of the ten counted, and the same question written 20 different ways.

What is the most common wording of the salary expectations question?

Two wordings are used by 3 postings each — Please indicate your minimum annual salary expectations (Non-Union positions only): and What are your salary expectations?. Three more are used by 2 each — What is your desired pay rate?, What are your base salary expectations? and What is your expected salary?. The other 15 appear once each; the full list of 20 is in section 02.

Do the postings publish a salary band?

Some do. Seven annual bands and two hourly rates appear in this sample — from $45,000 – $55,000 up to $110,000 – $130,000 a year, and $37.47 – $46.84 and $40.49 – $60.73 an hour. Most postings publish nothing, so where a band exists it is the number to anchor to. It is a sample of one pipeline, not a market range.
