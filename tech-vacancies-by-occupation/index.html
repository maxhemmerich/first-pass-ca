Canada's technical job vacancies, occupation by occupation

The salary pages ask what a technical role pays. This one asks how many of those roles are open, and what employers offered to fill them. It takes the sixteen technical NOC 2021 unit groups this site already covers by pay and reads the other half of the same question out of Statistics Canada's Job Vacancy and Wage Survey — a different survey from the wage file — with the source and the read date on every figure.

03

What this page is, and what it is not

  • A single source. Every row above is a string out of one Statistics Canada table, 14-10-0444, from the Job Vacancy and Wage Survey and published under the Statistics Canada Open Licence. The CSV read here (14100444.csv, unpacked from 14100444-eng.zip) hashes to bce3068b6554f614cc898a4d5b87e378f928b65981dc7be30395e9e6e637a41b; the table page and the download are where a reader can confirm it.
  • Sixteen rows, chosen and named. The occupations are the unit groups this lane covers, picked to mirror the salary pages and listed by NOC code so the choice is visible. They are a slice of the file's hundreds of occupations, not the whole labour market.
  • A vacancy is an unfilled post. Statistics Canada counts a job only where the employer is taking steps to recruit someone from outside the organisation to fill it, so a post that is filled, or one nobody is chasing, is not counted. What the table reads is employer demand, not every opening there is.
  • The wage is the advertised one. The survey records the rate an employer posted, and the lower end of a range where a range was published. What a hire is actually paid is a different survey's question — the one the salary pages ask.
  • The quarters are not seasonally adjusted. These are the survey's own raw quarterly figures, so a July quarter and a January quarter are not like for like. Read them as counts on the date printed.
  • Occupations and an economy, not a job ad. Nothing here names an employer or a single opening, and none of it is advice. No part of this page is drawn out of the paid pack.

The survey's other axis is job vacancies industry by industry. Its vacancy-rate and payroll-employee measures for these occupations sit in the same table and are left off here so each row lines up with the salary pages. Statistics Canada publishes the CSV for reuse with attribution; the links above are the whole source.
