Canada's technical job openings, projected to 2033

The salary pages ask what a technical role pays; the vacancy pages ask how many are open today. This one asks a third question: how many openings the federal projection model expects over the next decade, and how many people it expects to compete for them. It reads the sixteen technical NOC 2021 unit groups this site already covers out of ESDC's Canadian Occupational Projection System — a projection model on its own dataset, not the wage file and not the vacancy survey — with the source and the read date on every figure.

03

Scope, source, and what it is not

  • One dataset, quoted. Every cell above is a string out of a single ESDC dataset, Canadian Occupational Projection System (COPS), 2024–2033 projections, published under the Open Government Licence – Canada. The CSV read here (summary_sommaire_2024_2033_noc2021.csv) hashes to acf2a07aa11992d59e786931e88bb7ed876eee1d29336b27081e3f4151dcb35e; the dataset page and the download are where a reader can confirm it.
  • Sixteen codes, shown and cited. The rows are the NOC unit groups this site already prices, chosen to line up with the salary pages and printed with their codes so the pick is checkable. The file carries hundreds of occupations; this is one slice of them.
  • A projection, not a count. These figures come from a model that projects flows of job openings and job seekers ten years forward from assumptions about growth, retirement and immigration. They are not observed openings and not a promise; the vacancy pages print what employers were advertising on a real date.
  • Openings are not adverts. An opening here is a post the model expects to be created or vacated over the decade, including replacement for retirements and emigration. It is not the same as a vacancy an employer is trying to fill this quarter.
  • Conditions are ESDC's. The Shortage, Balance and Surplus labels are the model's own assessment of labour-market tightness for the occupation, carried verbatim; they are not advice and not this site's judgement.
  • A trade and a decade, not a job ad. Nothing here names an employer or a single opening, and none of it is a promise of work. Nothing on this page is lifted from the paid pack.

The same sixteen occupations, priced today, are on tech salaries across Canada and average pay, by occupation; what is open right now is on job vacancies by occupation and job vacancies by industry. This page is the decade ahead, from a different dataset again.
