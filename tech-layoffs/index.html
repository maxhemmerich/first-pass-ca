Canada's technical layoffs, counted

Every other reference on this site measures the market you are about to enter. This one measures the people who have just left it. It is not a list of announced job cuts; it is the benefit that follows one — the number of people in each technical occupation drawing regular Employment Insurance, counted by Statistics Canada and broken out by the occupation of the claim. The page prints, for the sixteen technical NOC 2021 unit groups this lane covers, that count at the latest reference period and month by month across the year, each figure the file's own, with the source and the date it was read.

03

What this page counts, and what it does not

  • One programme, one table. Everything above comes from a single Statistics Canada table, 14-10-0455, from the Employment Insurance Statistics programme and published under the Statistics Canada Open Licence. The CSV read here (14100455.csv, unpacked from 14100455-eng.zip) hashes to 87fda59b15269de99e3f6b5f631a50bce412a225b8a36ddf92bffbc13d3a31cf; the table page and the download are where a reader can confirm every figure.
  • A claimant, not an announcement. This is the administrative count of people actually drawing regular benefits. It is a different thing from the number of layoffs a company announces, because a separation only lands here once it happens and the person qualifies and applies. The page therefore lags the news, and undercounts it.
  • Counted under the last job, not the current one. A claimant is filed under the occupation of the work they lost. Someone retraining into a different field still counts under the old one, so this is a reading of where job losses landed, not of who is in the field now.
  • Small numbers move a lot. Several of these occupations number in the low hundreds nationally, so a month-to-month change of a few dozen people is noise as much as signal. Compare the same month across years and treat the direction, not the single reading.
  • Unadjusted for season. The programme publishes raw monthly counts, so winter is routinely higher than summer for reasons that have nothing to do with a particular occupation. No seasonal adjustment has been applied here or anywhere on this page.
  • A snapshot, and not advice. The table describes occupations and a country, not an employer or a job ad, and nothing on it is guidance of any kind. Whatever is inside the paid document, it is not the source of anything printed here.

The demand side of the same question — what employers are hiring for — is on the vacancy page, and the decade ahead on the projection page. Read together, the three say how many of a role are open, how many of it the government expects, and how many of it have just come loose. The whole of the figures above is that one open-licensed file, reproduced with the attribution the licence asks for.
